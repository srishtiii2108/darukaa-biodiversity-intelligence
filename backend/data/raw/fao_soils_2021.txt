Soil organic carbon (SOC) is a critical component of soil health and ecosystem resilience. In semi-arid regions characterized by low and irregular rainfall (under 600mm annually), conventional monoculture practices, such as continuous wheat cultivation, rapidly deplete SOC to critical levels (often below 0.8%). This depletion destroys soil aggregate stability and microbial diversity. Integrating agroforestry and legume-based cover crops can reverse this degradation. According to FAO studies, transitioning to agroforestry increases SOC by 15-25% over a 3-5 year period. The deep root systems of woody perennials break hardpan soil structures, significantly enhancing soil moisture retention and water infiltration rates by up to 30%. This combined effect of higher SOC and improved moisture provides a resilient microhabitat that supports microbial diversity, restores above-ground pollinator populations, and prevents severe soil erosion. Establishing this direct link between soil carbon management and biodiversity conservation is essential for climate-smart agriculture.
